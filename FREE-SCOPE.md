# Free access and choosing a resource

Start with the reading job: hearing and blending sounds, reading words, reading fluently, understanding a text, or analysing an argument. A story read aloud can support vocabulary and discussion; it does not demonstrate that the child can decode the text independently. Use phonics texts matched to sounds already taught by the school. Ask the teacher about persistent difficulty rather than treating a website as an assessment.

## What free means here

Public pages, a limited free collection, a free computer version, and library-funded member access have different boundaries. The directory names each boundary. Paid trials, Reading Eggs subscriptions, paid British Council classes, the UFLI manual and paid app versions are not counted as free resources. For library services, check residency, card eligibility and the specific collection before promising access.

## A short home routine

An adult previews one text. The child reads a manageable section, with support for words beyond taught knowledge. Ask one literal question and one question requiring a clue. Return to the text together and finish with one accurate sentence about its meaning. Keep a note of the actual response, not a score invented from time spent online. Offer older EAL learners topics appropriate to their age at an accessible language level.

Choose school-preferred Australian spelling and discuss regional variants. Adult-managed account setup is needed where stated. This review did not register children, test member access, audit advertising or establish complete curriculum coverage. External services may change. See [official evidence](SOURCES.md).
