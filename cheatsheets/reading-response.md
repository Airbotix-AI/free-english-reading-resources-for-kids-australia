# Reading response: answer with evidence

Airbotix original sharing sheet · 19 October 2026 · suggested Years 3–8, selected by reading level. Fictional worked example, not a student result or an exam-approved response formula.

| Step | What to do | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Read the question | Identify whether it asks for a fact, a summary, an inference or an explanation of language. | Answer the actual question. |
| Make a point | Write one direct, specific answer. | Avoid a vague opening about the whole story. |
| Find evidence | Select a short exact quotation or a precise detail. | Check the original wording and context. |
| Explain | Say how that evidence supports the point. | Do not simply repeat the quotation. |
| Recheck | Separate what the text states from what you infer. | A possible interpretation is not a proven fact. |

## Complete example

> When the rain began, Leo folded his map and moved his books away from the open window. He waited inside until the path stopped shining with water.

Question: Why does Leo probably wait inside? Use evidence.

Model answer: Leo probably waits to avoid getting himself and his books wet. He moves his books away from the open window when rain starts, then waits until the path is no longer shiny with water. Those details suggest wet weather changes his plan. The text does not say he is afraid of rain, so that stronger claim would need more evidence.

For an unfamiliar word, read the whole sentence, propose a meaning and test whether it fits. For a decoding difficulty, use taught sound patterns and ask an adult for help rather than guessing from pictures. Follow the question and the school rubric; a paragraph scaffold does not guarantee marks.
